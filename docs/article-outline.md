# InfoQ article — outline (practitioner decision guide)

Audience: practitioners. They want the decision rule and the numbers, not the taxonomy.
Lead with a finding, not the ladder.

1. **The problem** — everyone stacks reliability layers; nobody knows which pay.
2. **The ladder** — 7 rungs, one concrete paragraph each.
3. **How we measured** — task, dataset(s), cost model. Short; trusted but skimmed. [A]
4. **The numbers** — frontier, the knee, Δquality/Δcost per rung. The core. [A]
5. **The flip** — optimal rung changes under cheap-error vs expensive-error economics. Headline. [B]
6. **The tool** — configurator, "pick your rung," screenshot/link.
7. **Decision rule** — takeaway table: cheap errors → stop at X; expensive → go to Y. [B]

Discipline: write each figure's caption the day you generate the figure.
A caption you can't write = a result that isn't clear yet.
