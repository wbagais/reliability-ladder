The Reliability Ladder · article raw material

What the corpus said about the plan

Seven reliability layers wrapped around an LLM, and a measurement of what each one actually buys. This is the layer underneath the article: the numbers worth quoting, and every place the plan and the data disagreed.

Corpus CADEC v3 · 1,250 posts · 9,111 gold mentions Vocabulary SNOMED CT AU1000036_20260731 Model calls zero
01Thesis

The thesis, in one paragraph

Every LLM agent is roughly 10% model and 90% harness, and that harness gets built by intuition: teams stack retries, validators, judges, voting and human review because each one sounds like it should help. The seven layers are known. What nobody publishes is the measured trade-off per layer — what each buys, in which currency, and where it stops being worth paying for. That curve is the contribution; everything else exists to keep it clean.

Two tracks. Track 1 is the data-agnostic ladder with SROIE receipts as its demo dataset — shipped, with a full 60×10 run measured. Track 2 is the CADEC pharmacovigilance instance, where rung 1 stops being a format check and becomes a real validation gate against a clinical terminology. Owner A's half of track 2 is built and measured; the model-facing rungs are outstanding.

02Findings
Ranked by how quotable they are, not by when they were found.

The findings worth a pull-quote

A gate's leniency setting decides whether it protects you or lies to you

19.0% of planted near-miss codes are put in ACCEPT by the intuitive lexical setting — records the validation gate actively vouches for. The strict setting puts 0.1% there.

Rung 1 sorts each record into REJECT (provably wrong), ACCEPT (the vocabulary uses these very words) or BAND (plausible, unverifiable). The ACCEPT/BAND divider is a string comparison between the quoted patient text and SNOMED's terms for the predicted code, and it has one obvious knob: exact equality, or token containment.

Containment is the intuitive choice. Patients write “bit drowsy” where SNOMED says “Drowsy”; being strict looks pedantic, and being lenient lifts the share of a perfect answer set that rung 1 settles for free from 43.1% to 54.5%.

Then we planted near-miss codes — a real, active clinical finding sharing its head word with the right one, which is the confusion a normalisation model actually makes — and asked what the gate did with them.

Lexical settingNear-misses caughtNear-misses put in ACCEPTACCEPT lane on gold
contained0.1%19.0%54.5%
exact0.1%0.1%43.1%

Neither setting catches near-misses — deterministic checks can't, and the plan said so. But the lenient one endorses one in five, because the span text is a subset of the wrong concept's term. It doesn't fail to protect you; it hands you false confidence.

We changed the default to exact and paid eleven points of free settlement for it. The rule worth writing down: for any validation gate, measure the permissive setting's false-vouch rate on planted near-misses before you ship it. “Be lenient with colloquial input” is a reasonable instinct that here converts a gate into an endorsement machine.

Deterministic checks are exact, and blind

Six corruption classes, one at a time, 8,666 gold records each, zero model calls.

Planted errorRung 1 rejects
Hallucinated code — in no release1.000
Span shifted two characters1.000
Fabricated quote1.000
Real code, wrong branch of the hierarchy1.000
Random plausible clinical finding0.000
Near-miss clinical finding0.001

Deterministic checks are not “pretty good” at their job. On the classes they are designed for they are exact, at zero model cost — and on the one class that matters most, a real, well-typed, wrong code, they are blind by construction, because the code is not in the source text and nothing mechanical can put it there.

That reframes the ladder, and it beats “a stack of improvements”: rung 1 is a free, exact filter for a specific set of failure modes, and everything above it is a paid resolver for the one class the filter cannot see. The economic question is not whether rung 1 helps — it does, for free — but what the paid rungs charge for the residue.

Over half of a perfect answer set is unverifiable

Worth stating as a planning tool: run your deterministic layer over your answer key first — the BAND fraction is your bill.

The metric trap (track 1, SROIE)

0.938 → 0.772 Yield across the judge rung, while accuracy rose 0.938 → 0.960. The layer improved the metric most teams watch and produced 17% fewer correct fields.

Accuracy is a ratio over answered items, so any layer that withholds answers raises it for free. Yield — the share of all field slots that came out correct — is the honest headline. Yield by rung on the full run: 0.938 / 0.938 / 0.932 / 0.934 / 0.772 / 0.772 / 0.988. Only the human rung beat the bare model.

This is the most transferable finding in the project: it costs nothing to adopt and it invalidates a lot of published layer comparisons.

The stack is worse than its best layer

Voting alone yields 0.946 — above the bare model and above every cumulative rung except the human one. Voting inside the cumulative stack yields 0.772, because it inherits the judge's damage. Judge alone yields 0.905, below baseline. Cumulative stacking is the wrong default; compose deliberately.

Self-referential layers are inert against a confidently wrong model

Format checks, self-confidence abstention and self-correction moved accuracy by 0.000 on track 1's smoke run and barely more at scale — every rung-0 error was a wrong value at self-reported confidence 0.9–1.0. The first gain came from the first layer with an independent signal.

Track 2 predicts the same shape for a different reason and will test it: rung 3 fires only on a rung 1 failure, and the gate's false-rejection floor is 0.13%. Self-correction needs a stated fact, and a validation gate that is honest about not knowing produces very few of them.

03Iterations
The differentiator. Most measurement pieces present the finished number; the value is in the corrections.

What we changed, and why

Rung 1 as the plan specifies it rejected 9.3% of the gold standard. A validation gate with a 9% false-positive rate does not measure a model — it manufactures errors, and every rung above inherits them. Three causes, all fixed.

1 · The plan said negation was a free win. The corpus said it rejects 4.7% of correct answers.

Plan
Negation gets its own boxed section: a cue list and a window catch “so far no gastric problems” extracted as a reported reaction, at zero cost, logged as its own rejection reason.
Data
The check rejected 427 gold-correct mentions, from two independent causes. First, the plan's worked example is post ARTHROTEC.1, and CADEC annotates gastric problems in that very sentence as an ADR coded 162076009 — CADEC annotates a mention regardless of polarity. Second, NegEx scope misfires on forum prose: “I can't describe the horrible stomach pain”, “I can finally clean my house without pain”, “doctors deny that there is a connection between joint pain … and Lipitor”. Each negates something; in none of them is it the mention.
Change
Demoted from a rejection to an audit flag. The detector still runs and the rate is still reported — polarity is a real safety class — but under this answer key it may not reject. negation_action: "reject" reproduces the plan as written, and costs 427 mentions.
Why it matters
The cleanest example of a check that is correct and still wrong to enforce, because the ground truth encodes a different policy. You do not find this in the plan; you find it by running your check against the answer key.

2 · The semantic-type check was rejecting retired concepts, not wrong ones

Data
“Is this code a descendant of |Clinical finding|?” rejected 416 gold-correct mentions — and 413 of them for a reason with nothing to do with semantics. |Knee pain| (63 mentions), |Weakness of limb| (53), |Mentally dull| (38), |Bloating symptom| (34): all clinically right, all retired from SNOMED since CADEC was coded in 2015.
Mechanism
When SNOMED retires a concept it also retires that concept's is-a relationships. A walk over active relationships cannot place a retired concept anywhere — and “cannot place” was being read as “is in the wrong branch”.
Change
The index stores the |Clinical finding| descendant set twice: over active is-a rows (129,675 concepts) and over every is-a row ever published (177,603). finding_status() returns finding / not_finding / unknown, and rung 1 may reject only on a positive not_finding. Absence of evidence is not evidence of a wrong slot.
Residual
3 rejections in 9,111 — and all three are the check working: |Eruption|, a morphologic abnormality, coded for “Abdominal rash”; an observable entity coded for “abdominal pressure”.

3 · “Does the code exist?” is ambiguous, and the two readings differ by 11%

Data
Of the 1,046 distinct SNOMED codes CADEC uses, in the 2026-07 release: 927 active, 115 present but inactive (11%), 4 absent entirely. Reading exists as “active in the current release” rejects 6.9% of the gold standard.
Change
exists() means present in the release, active or not; inactivity is recorded as an audit fact rather than a verdict. reject_inactive is a manifest setting, because it is exactly the kind of choice that silently moves the headline number.
General point
Vocabulary drift is a measurement artefact that looks exactly like a model error. Any benchmark that normalises to a versioned terminology and does not pin the release is reporting the terminology's release notes as its model's performance.

The residual 12 are 5 codes absent from the release, 4 genuine typos in CADEC's own annotations, and 3 real gold miscodings. The method is the transferable part, and it costs nothing: replay your deterministic layer over your answer key. Every rejection there is false by construction, so you get the gate's false-positive rate exactly, before any model output exists. None of the three causes would have been visible in model output — they would have shown up as a plausible-looking 9% rejection rate and been written up as a finding.

The plan's record shape encoded the claim its own safety constraint forbade

The plan's example record pairs drug_text with reaction_text in one object. Its own safety constraint says drug and reaction mentions are extracted independently and the system never emits “drug X causes Y”. Pairing them makes every output a causal claim by construction, so the unit became one record = one mention.

The same reasoning killed a second thing: CADEC's most common entity label is ADR — adverse drug reaction — which is a causal attribution made by a human annotator. Asking a model to reproduce that label is asking for exactly the claim the constraint forbids. The four clinical types collapse to reaction; only reaction-vs-drug is asked for or scored.

Article angle: safety constraints written as prose get contradicted by the data model three pages later. The ones that hold are the ones with no way to express the forbidden thing — a missing interface, not a warning.

That 0.04% is the floor the cheapest check on the ladder can never get below. Every benchmark has one; almost nobody reports it.

Process choices that paid for themselves

  • The fixture gate caught our own wrong expectation on its first run. The failing case was one we had asserted should ACCEPT: “little blurred vision”, coded 246636008. SNOMED's terms for that concept are Foggy / Hazy / Misty / Cloudy vision — it never uses the word “blurred”. Correct code, zero lexical evidence, correctly BAND.
  • Every open choice became a manifest setting, not a default argument. Four settings in rung 1 each move the rejection rate by 5–15 points. A number produced by a hard-coded opinion is not reproducible even by its author.
  • Splits are by document, not by mention — mentions from one post share its wording and its annotator — and stratified by drug family, because CADEC is 80% Lipitor and the human-agreement ceiling we cite was measured on the other one.
  • Missing rungs are reported, never faked. Half a ladder honestly labelled is a result. The scorer is injected the same way, so accuracy columns are written empty rather than guessed.
  • An earlier incident (track 1): the app wrote every run to a single results file, so a 3-item run silently destroyed a finished 60-item benchmark — recovered for free by replaying from the call cache. If your eval harness caches model calls, results files are disposable; if it doesn't, one careless rerun costs you the experiment.
  • A finding we published to ourselves and retracted: on a 10-item smoke run, cross-prompt vote dispersion looked like a better abstention signal than self-reported confidence. At n=60 it isn't. Nine errors was too small a base.
04Structure
Six beats, practitioner voice.

Suggested article structure

Reordered from the original outline on purpose: lead with the finding that is cheapest for a reader to act on.

  1. Every agent is 90% harness, built by vibes. The hook. Name the seven layers in a paragraph each and move on — they are not the contribution.
  2. The metric trap. Short, concrete, immediately actionable, and it earns the reader's trust for the harder material.
  3. What a free layer actually buys. Exact on its own classes, blind on the interesting one, and 57% of even a perfect answer set left unverifiable. Reframe: free filter plus paid resolvers.
  4. The gate that vouches for the error. The strongest single number, and a process story: the obvious setting, the measurement, the reversal. This is where the piece earns its “we measured it”.
  5. The flip. Whether withholding is worth it depends entirely on your cost of a wrong answer versus a missing one — rung 1 below ~$2 per error, rung 2 at ~$5, human review from ~$10 up.
  6. Decision rules and the tool. Compose deliberately, not cumulatively. Calibrate the abstention gate per model before concluding the layer doesn't work. Run your deterministic layer over your answer key before you trust it. Measure the permissive setting's false-vouch rate.

06Limits

Limitations to state plainly

  • Track 2 is half a ladder. Rungs 0, 3, 4 and 5 are outstanding. Everything in section 2's first three findings is a property of the gate, measured against the gold standard and against planted errors — not a model result. Saying so makes the claim stronger, not weaker.
  • Track 1 is one model on one task. The contribution is the method and the shape, not a leaderboard.
  • Determinism was 1.000 at every rung on the local model — greedy decoding at temperature 0 is exactly reproducible, so that axis is free locally and only gets interesting on hosted APIs with batching nondeterminism. The cross-model run is not done.
  • Contamination. CADEC is public and from 2015, so it is almost certainly in pretraining. It inflates rung 0, which makes the ladder's gains look smaller — the conclusion is conservative. The v2 slice is the check, and is not run yet.
  • Human ceiling. Strict span agreement between CADEC's own annotators was about 68.7 on the diclofenac posts. A system near 0.69 strict span F1 is at the noise floor of the answer key, not underperforming.
  • The abstention target is thin at 60 test documents: 7 CONCEPT_LESS mentions in 393. Abstention accuracy cannot be separated from noise there.
  • Near-miss corruption is synthetic. The pool is built from concepts sharing a head word — a proxy for the confusions a normalisation model makes, not a sample of them. The 19% is a property of the check under a plausible error model, and should be quoted that way.
